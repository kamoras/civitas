# Member score v6.19

## Housekeeping votes don't count as breaks with the party

**Before (v6.18).** Every roll call the parties split on (at least 65% of
one party voting Yea, at most 35% of the other) counted toward party
loyalty, including quorum calls, motions to adjourn, the House's previous
question, and motions to table or to recommit.

**Now.** Those questions never count for or against the party
(`normalize_votes.is_housekeeping`, read from the roll call's question in
`stamp_roll_call_outcome`). They split on party lines as a matter of
course: a motion to recommit is the minority's messaging vote, the previous
question the majority's hold on the floor. Rule votes, cloture and
nominations still count, because they decide whether a bill reaches the
floor, whether it gets a vote, and who serves. This narrower rule was
chosen over excluding all procedural votes and nominations, which would have
removed about half of the House's party-line votes and three quarters of
the Senate's.

**Measured on the 119th Congress's roll calls.** 137 of 446 House
party-line roll calls (31%: 65 motions to recommit, 61 previous-question
votes, motions to table and to adjourn) and 19 of 641 Senate ones (3%) no
longer count. One House member's six breaks included one of these, a motion
to recommit.

The seat's expected break rate is measured from the same votes every run,
so the norm each member is compared with changes with them.
