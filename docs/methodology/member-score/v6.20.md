# Member score v6.20

## Breaks toward the other party, over the whole Congress, each measure once

**Before (v6.19).** Constituent Alignment's break rate was read off the
member's stored votes: a sample of the chamber's latest 120 roll calls plus
key bills. Every vote against most of the party counted, and a nominee's
cloture and confirmation votes counted as two.

**Now.** The rate is each member's party-line record over every roll call
the chamber recorded this Congress (`party_line_record.party_line_records`,
stored per member in `party_line_record`, Alembic 0010), with two rules:

- **Only breaks toward the other party count.** On that roll call, the
  party's members who broke must sit, on average, nearer the other party
  (mean DW-NOMINATE first dimension) than the party does. A vote against
  the party from its flank (hardliners voting down their own party's bill)
  is listed on the scorecard as from the flank and not counted: how far
  toward the flank a member sits is already scored, by position
  congruence.
- **Each measure counts once.** Cloture, confirmation, motion to proceed,
  passage and the like on one nomination or bill are one decision. An
  amendment, a motion to commit or to waive, is its own question.

Housekeeping questions (v6.19) and the majority leader's reconsider switch
stay excluded. Until a member's record is measured (the first pipeline run
after this release, or a member the roll calls name differently), the score
reads their stored votes as before.

**Why.** Tim Burchett (R-TN-2) scored 8: 6 breaks in 76 sampled votes
(7.9%), against 1.1% for Republicans in seats like his. Over the whole
Congress it was 15 of 304 (4.9%), and 14 of the 15 came from the Republican
right flank. Counting only breaks toward Democrats, each measure once, he
breaks on 2 of 298 measures (0.7%), the House Republican median.

**Evidence.** Tested against election results in
`docs/research/constituent-alignment.md`, sections 11 and 12. Counting only
centerward breaks predicts Senate general-election vote share
(−0.042 per point, t = −3.0, against −0.039, t = −2.6) and contested House
primary share (+0.057, t = 2.6, against +0.053, t = 2.4) at least as well as
counting all breaks. Counting each measure once changes neither (−0.042,
t = −3.0; +0.056, t = 2.5). It is adopted as the right unit: 37% of the
119th Senate's roll calls repeat a measure already voted on, against 5% in
the 101st.

The seat's expected rate and each party's scale are measured from the same
records every run, so the norm moves with them.
