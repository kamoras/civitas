# Member score v6.23

## Legislative Effectiveness: a member sworn in mid-Congress is compared with the share of it they served

**Before (v6.22).** The bill component (60% of Legislative Effectiveness)
compares a member's stage-normalized bill credit for the current Congress
with the median credit of members of the same majority/minority status. That
median is measured on members who have served the whole Congress so far. A
member who won a special election had the same bar, with no allowance for
arriving late.

**What went wrong.** Credit accumulates over the time a member has to
sponsor and advance bills. On 2026-09-29 the House Clerk's member list
(`clerk.house.gov/xml/lists/MemberData.xml`, `<sworn-date>`) showed 11
sitting members sworn in after the 119th Congress convened on January 3,
2025, six of them during 2026. Each was measured against colleagues with up
to 20 more months:

- Clay Fuller (GA-14, sworn in 2026-04-14) scored 17.7 on the bill component
  for 10 bills in five months, against the bar for 20 months.
- Everton Blair (GA-13, sworn in 2026-09-01) scored 38.4 for one bill
  introduced in his first four weeks. Without it he would have scored a
  neutral 50, because a member with no bills and under half a year in office
  is neutral. Introducing a bill lowered his score, which breaks the
  component's own rule that inaction never beats an attempt.

**Now.** The bar is prorated by the share of the Congress so far that the
member has served (`congress_exposure`): days since they were sworn in over
days since the Congress convened, up to the date the reference was measured
(`measured_on`, now stamped on every LES reference). A member seated on
January 3 has a share of 1 and is unaffected. So is any member whose date is
unknown. The breakdown names the date and the share.

The sworn-in date is read each House run from the Clerk's list
(`fetch/house_clerk.py`) and stored on the representative (`sworn_date`,
migration 0016). When the list can't be read, the date stored last is used.

The chamber median itself is unchanged. The 11 members are about 2.5% of
the House, and removing them would move the median by less than the
rounding in the breakdown.

**Not covered: the Senate.** No Senate source the pipeline reads gives a
sworn-in date. An appointee who takes a seat mid-Congress is still compared
with the full bar. None is seated in the 119th Congress: both 2025
appointees took their seats on January 21, 2025, 2.6% of the way in.

**Also in this version.**

- *Stage fallback.* When a bill's action history can't be fetched, its
  stage is read from its latest action. A latest action such as "Motion to
  proceed ... not agreed to" or "... Failed by the Yeas and Nays" was read
  as the chamber passing the bill, because it contains "agreed to" or
  "pass". Those now count as not passed.
- *Funding Independence guard.* The PAC share a campaign's size predicts
  (v6.22) is capped at 100%. A steep enough fit would otherwise put the
  smallest campaigns' expected share above 100%, where a member funded
  entirely by PACs would score above the typical 50. The current fits stay
  below the cap (House 65% at the smallest typical campaign), so no score
  moves.
