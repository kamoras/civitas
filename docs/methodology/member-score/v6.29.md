# Member score v6.29

## Senators: approval among the state's other-party voters and independents

### What changed

Constituent Alignment gains a part for senators:
`app/pipeline/analyze/constituent_approval.py`. From the Cooperative Election
Study (`app/data/ces_member_approval.json`, `scripts/fetch_ces_approval.py`),
each senator's approval among the state's respondents of the other party and
among independents (empirical-Bayes shrunk, as stored) is compared, in
log-odds, with the typical senator of the same party (the file's prior),
corrected for the state's lean within party (independents in safe states lean
toward the majority: r = +0.29 before the correction), and the two are
averaged. Scaled by the population's standard deviation and mapped
`50 + 50 tanh(z / 1.5)`, it moves the score by `0.30 x (part - 50)`.

    CA = 0.70 vote + 0.30 position + 0.30 (approval - 50)    (clamped 0-100)

A senator the survey didn't rate (first seated after it was fielded) adds
nothing. The House is not scored on it.

### Why these groups

Constituents' approval follows how their member votes, and most among the
other party and independents; the member's own party rates mostly by party
(Ansolabehere & Kuriwaki 2022, AJPS 66(1); Ansolabehere & Jones 2010, AJPS
54(3)). Own-party approval here ran the other way (-0.05 with the personal
vote below).

### Measurements (2026-10-07)

Reliability, same member in two waves (CES 2022 and 2024, different
respondents), log-odds deviation from the typical same-party member:

| Group | Senate | House |
|---|---|---|
| Own party | 0.63 | 0.24 |
| Other party | 0.55 | 0.19 |
| Independents | 0.59 | 0.21 |

Validity: personal vote (the senator's two-party share minus their party's
presidential two-party share in the state, same election; 2022 against the
2020 and 2024 average), each part measured over the Congress before the
election (Voteview roll calls: break rate beyond the seat's prediction;
Nokken-Poole position toward the seat's center), approval from the wave
fielded with the election, per standard deviation, bootstrap 95% CI:

| Sample | Vote part | Position | Approval |
|---|---|---|---|
| Senate 2022+2024, n = 45 | -0.84 (-1.81, +0.06) | +1.27 (+0.24, +2.00) | +0.77 (+0.22, +1.52) |
| House 2024, n = 326 | +0.24 (-0.11, +0.54) | +0.11 (-0.23, +0.46) | +0.38 (+0.14, +0.63) |

A first test that set the sitting Congress's parts against 2024's elections
gave the House vote part a negative coefficient (-0.29); measured over the
Congress before the election it is positive (+0.24, simple r +0.17), so that
was the timing, not the part. Separately, with every current senator's
current parts and the 2024 approval, approval's coefficient was +0.99
(+0.37, +1.73) and it replicated in the 2022 wave alone (r = +0.29, 84
senators).

### Why the House is not scored

A district holds about 24 other-party and 29 independent respondents; the
shrinkage leaves 37% and 7% of a member's figure their own respondents', and
the same member's figure two years apart correlates 0.09 to 0.24. Approval
does carry signal across the House in aggregate (+0.38 above), but an
individual member's reading is mostly noise, so it would rank members by
sampling luck. It stays on the scorecard as context.

### Why it is added, not given a share

Given a share (54% vote, 23% position, 23% approval), the part, centred on 50
for a typical senator, pulled every rated senator down: Constituent
Alignment's typical value is 85 (70% of 100 plus 30% of 50), so a typical
rated senator would have scored about 77, eight points below an unrated
senator or a House member. Measured on the October 2026 data the share
version lowered rated senators by 4.2 points on average. Added around the
typical senator, a typical rating changes nothing, as a member with no
position yet sits at 50 on the position part.

### Effect (October 2026 data)

Among the 82 rated senators: mean change +0.2, mean absolute change 5.4,
range -15 to +15. Unrated senators and all House members unchanged.

### Data currency

The survey runs every two years; the current wave was fielded October and
November 2024, before the 119th Congress. Its two-year reliability (above) is
what makes a 2024 reading meaningful for the current term. Regenerate the
file with `scripts/fetch_ces_approval.py` when the 2026 wave is released.
