# Member score v6.35

Constituent Alignment's seat expectation bends at a swing seat only when
the bend predicts the chamber's own members better out of sample.

## The rule it replaces

Each run fits, per chamber and per party, the break rate members show at
each seat lean (`compute_constituent_reference`): a fractional logit on the
party-signed lean, with an extra slope term for seats leaning to the other
party (the kink, `b_opposed`). From v6.13 to v6.34 the kink was fitted
whenever five or more of the party's members held such seats.

In the 119th Congress five was too few to tell a real bend from noise:

| Chamber, party | Opposed seats | Fitted `b_opposed` | Leave-one-out gain | Its standard error |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| House, D | 7 | -3.34 | -0.018 | 0.193 |
| Senate, D | 7 | +3.76 | -0.014 | 0.024 |
| House, R | 3 | not fitted | -2.156 | 2.276 |
| Senate, R | 1 | not fitted | +0.029 | 0.125 |

The gain is the kinked fit's leave-one-out quasi-log-likelihood minus the
single slope's, summed over the party's full-confidence members, measured
on a copy of production. In the House the bend doubled Democrats'
opposed-seat slope. A bootstrap over members (300 resamples) put its 95%
interval at -12.1 to +0.3, and the expected break rate for a Democrat in
an R+5 seat at 59% (28% to 96%). On the opposed and swing-seat members
themselves the kinked fit predicted worse than the straight one (-0.14).
The steepened slope made those members' scores the most sensitive to their
seat's lean: up to 39 Constituent Alignment points per PVI point. In the
Senate the bend ran the other way, flattening the line for the seven
Democrats in Republican-leaning states.

## The rule now

`select_opposed_kink` fits both forms and keeps the kink only when its
leave-one-out quasi-log-likelihood beats the single slope's by more than
one standard error of the per-member differences: the one-standard-error
rule (Breiman et al. 1984; Hastie, Tibshirani & Friedman 2009, section 7.10).
Leave-one-out has no random folds, so a population always gets the same
answer. Each fit records the comparison under `kink` in
`constituent_reference.json`. The reference's statistic stamp changes
(`/kink=loo-1se`), so a reference measured under the old rule is not scored
against. The bundled pre-first-run fallback is regenerated from the same copy
of production by `scripts/calibrate_constituent_reference.py`.

On synthetic chambers the rule keeps a real bend: one like the 108th House,
29 opposed seats breaking far more than the aligned slope predicts. It drops
a bend that isn't there: seven opposed seats on a straight line.

The presidential-support expectation, which fitted its kink by the same count,
now uses the same selection. On the same copy it drops the House
Democrats' bend (gain +43 votes, standard error 65, below the margin); 66
of 433 representatives' expected rates move by more than a point, up to 35
points for the opposed-seat members. It is shown, not scored.

## Score effect

Re-scored on the copy of production. The before scores reproduce every stored
score (100 of 100 senators, 433 of 433 representatives).

| Chamber | Moved | Mean change, Constituent Alignment | Largest | Largest, overall | 5+ points |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Senate | 37 of 100 | 0.93 | 9 | 3.0 | 4 |
| House | 190 of 433 | 0.98 | 18 | 5.9 | 18 |

Only Democrats move (Republicans had no bend in either chamber). Their mean
rises 0.36 points in the Senate and 0.78 in the House. The largest moves
are opposed and swing-seat Democrats, now compared with the party's single
line.

Sensitivity to the seat's lean (Constituent Alignment points per PVI point,
absolute):

| Chamber | p90 before | after | p99 before | after | max before | after |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Senate | 7.8 | 6.5 | 14.6 | 14.6 | 14.6 | 14.6 |
| House | 7.7 | 6.7 | 16.8 | 14.3 | 39.3 | 19.7 |

The Senate maximum is a Republican in a Democratic-leaning state, where no
bend was fitted before or after.

The ground-truth gate's population probes pass: share beyond saturation
0.110 (Senate) and 0.102 (House) against the 0.20 limit, largest share at
one score 0.050 and 0.042 against 0.5. The Senate's shadow re-score
(`scripts/rescore.py`) passes 14 of 14 derived checks.
