# Member score v6.27

## Constituent Alignment: a roll-call position counts at the strength its votes support

**Before (v6.26).** Position congruence (30% of Constituent Alignment)
compares a member's congress-specific Nokken-Poole position with what a
same-party member of a seat with that lean typically holds. Every position
counted in full, however many roll calls it was estimated from. Voteview's
placeholder for a member it could not scale (0, 0) was read as a centrist
position.

**What went wrong.** A position estimated from a few roll calls is mostly
noise. Re-locating members of Congresses 101–118 from random subsets of
their own votes gives these median errors:

| Votes | Senate | House |
|---|---|---|
| 10 | 0.143 | 0.157 |
| 40 | 0.051 | 0.059 |
| 160 | 0.021 | 0.026 |

For scale, the score runs from 50 to 0 over 0.22–0.26. The errors are also
heavy-tailed: a handful of party-line votes cannot place a member within
their party at all. Members sworn in late, members who left early, and
everyone early in a Congress were scored on these estimates at full
strength.

**Now.**

- **Each position counts at the strength its votes support:** the
  component is weighted toward 50 by n / (n + n0), where n is the member's
  scaled roll calls this Congress.
  - The weight is the measured slope of the score a full record gives on
    the score n votes give, so it is the best linear prediction of where
    the member really sits.
  - `scripts/calibrate_position_confidence.py` measures it by combining
    those sampling errors with the sitting Congress's spread of positions,
    under the score as it ships. It writes
    `app/data/position_confidence.json`.
  - n0 is 23.5 votes in the Senate and 31.7 in the House. Party-line
    voting is heavier in the House, so a House vote says less about where
    a member sits within their party.
- **No cutoff.** The seat fits and the scale use every member: a thin
  position is noisy, not biased.
- **Missing or unusable positions:**
  - A position with no reported vote count scores 50, since Voteview gives
    no count for a member it has barely scaled.
  - A placeholder is not a position and is left out.
  - The weight does not apply to the DW-NOMINATE fallback, a career
    position that this Congress's count doesn't describe.
- **Current term only.** Early in a Congress, every member is read on the
  new Congress's own few votes, and so close to 50. A section left from an
  earlier Congress is never read (principle 6).
- **Flank breaks.** The rule that decides whether a break goes toward the
  other party now weights the defectors' and the party's mean positions by
  reliability too.
- **Ingest gate fix.** The House's member-count gate counted rows, and by
  October 2026 the 119th House export had 451 of them (delegates and
  mid-Congress replacements) against a limit of 450. Every House refresh
  since then had failed and kept older data. The gate now counts seats.

**What was tested and kept.** The same study tested the two other ways this
component differs from the vote part. Neither changed:

- **50 at the expectation, not 100.** Across House general elections
  1994–2010 and Senate general elections 1990–2024, the linear shape
  predicted the incumbent's vote share (t=5.2 and t=2.5). Peaking it at the
  expectation, as the vote part does, predicted nothing (t=−0.4, t=−0.7).
  Sitting nearer the seat's center earns credit with voters, so it scores
  above 50. A typical member scoring about 50 here is expected.
- **One scale for both parties.** One scale per party predicts elections
  equally well, and the test of which unit voters respond to found no
  difference between the parties under either. The pooled scale stays.
  Party averages are equal under both (over every Senate since 1989, 50.5 D
  and 51.6 R). Republican positions spread more widely around their
  expectation, though, so more Republicans sit at 0 and at 100.

The study is `docs/research/constituent-alignment.md`, section 14
(reproduce with `scripts/research_constituent_alignment.py`). The section
also records why the first draft of this change, a cutoff of 81 votes from
a different noise proxy, was replaced. The cutoff was not robust (anywhere
from 11 to 157 votes under resampling), and it answered the wrong question.

**Effect on the 119th Congress** (Voteview exports of 2026-10-03):

| | Senate | House |
|---|---|---|
| Mean change in position congruence | 0.59 | 1.55 |
| Mean change in Constituent Alignment | 0.18 | 0.47 |
| Members counted at under 90% | 3 | 15 |

The largest changes are for thin records:

- Two recently sworn-in representatives have no count yet. They move from
  23.3 and 31.1 to 50 on the component, which is +8.0 and +5.7 on
  Constituent Alignment.
- A representative with 77 votes moves from 93.2 to 80.6.
- Members who have left, each scored from 1 to 39 votes, move by up to 49
  points.

Separately, the House's positions refresh again now that the gate is fixed.
