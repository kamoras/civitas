# Member score v6.27

## Constituent Alignment: a roll-call position counts at the strength its votes support

**Before (v6.26).** Position congruence (30% of Constituent Alignment)
compares a member's congress-specific Nokken-Poole position with what a
same-party member of a seat with that lean typically holds. Every position
counted in full, however many roll calls it was estimated from. Voteview's
placeholder for a member it could not scale (0, 0) was read as a centrist
position.

**What went wrong.** A position estimated from a few roll calls is mostly
noise. Members sworn in late, members who left early, and everyone early in
a Congress were scored on such estimates at full strength.

**Now.**

- **Each position counts at the strength its votes support.** The
  component's distance from 50 is scaled by n / (n + n0), where n is the
  member's scaled roll calls this Congress.
  - The weight is measured on Voteview's own positions:
    - The data are members with a thin record in one Congress and a full
      one in the next, or the reverse (108 such pairs in Congresses
      101–119).
    - The fit regresses the full position on the thin one, both read from
      the party's center.
    - It separates that from ordinary Congress-to-Congress drift using
      8,099 pairs of full records.
  - n0 rises with how party-line a Congress votes, since each vote then
    says less about where a member sits within their party. It is fitted
    as a function of the share of roll calls on which the parties'
    majorities voted opposite ways, and read at the sitting Congress's
    share.
    - House: 64 votes (90% interval 38–104).
    - Senate: 76 (45–119). The 119th Senate (0.92) is more party-line than
      any Congress measured, so it is read at the edge of the data, 0.756.
  - A full record of about 560 votes counts 0.90.
  - `scripts/calibrate_position_confidence.py` writes these values to
    `app/data/position_confidence.json`. Rerun it each Congress.
- **Positions with no count.** Voteview sometimes publishes a position
  with no vote count, for a member it has barely scaled. Such a position
  reads as resting on none: in recent Congresses it showed no measurable
  weight.
- **No cutoff.** The seat fits and the scale use every member: a thin
  position is noisy, not biased.
- **Members without a position.** Voteview's placeholder is not a
  position and is left out. A member the current Congress's data has no
  position for sits at a neutral 50, as a position from no votes would. The
  component is no longer dropped for them, which had let the vote part
  carry their whole score.
- **Current term only.** Each Voteview section records its Congress, and a
  position counts only for roll calls of the same Congress (principle 6).
  - After January 3, until the new Congress's Voteview export passes the
    ingest gates, the component is left out for everyone, as when no data
    exists.
  - From then on, members are read on the new Congress's own votes,
    weighted toward 50 while those are few.
  - A stored score's "show the math" keeps reading the positions it was
    scored on until the next run rescores it.
  - The weight applies to the DW-NOMINATE fallback too, since this
    Congress's count is the current term's evidence.
- **Flank breaks.** The rule that decides whether a break goes toward the
  other party now weights the defectors' and the party's mean positions by
  reliability. On the 119th Congress's roll calls this changes none of
  2,351 Senate breaks and 2 of 6,055 House breaks.
- **Ingest fixes.**
  - The House's gate counted members with a position against a limit of
    450. By October 2026 the 119th House export had 451, counting
    delegates and mid-Congress replacements, so every House refresh had
    failed and kept older data. The gate now counts seats (380–435 House,
    45–50 Senate), plus a floor on seated members.
  - Exports that write party and district codes as floats ("200.0", as the
    115th–117th do) failed to parse. They parse now.

**What was tested and kept.** The same study tested the two other ways this
component differs from the vote part. Neither changed:

- **50 at the expectation, not 100.** Across House general elections
  1994–2010 and Senate general elections 1990–2024, the linear shape
  predicted the incumbent's vote share (t=5.2 and t=2.5). Peaking it at the
  expectation, as the vote part does, predicted nothing (t=−0.4, t=−0.5).
  Sitting nearer the seat's center earns credit with voters, so it scores
  above 50. A typical member scoring about 50 here is expected.
- **One scale for both parties.** One scale per party predicts elections
  equally well, and the test of which unit voters respond to found no
  difference between the parties under either. The pooled scale stays.
  Party averages are equal under both (over every Senate since 1989,
  unweighted, 50.5 D and 51.6 R). Republican positions spread more widely
  around their expectation, so more Republicans sit near the ends of the
  scale.

The study is `docs/research/constituent-alignment.md`, section 14
(reproduce with `scripts/research_constituent_alignment.py`). That section
also records two earlier drafts of this change and why they were replaced.
The first was a cutoff of 81 votes from a noise proxy, which resampling put
anywhere from 11 to 157. The second re-estimated positions from random
subsets of votes, which understates how noisy a real run of consecutive
votes is and pooled every era.

**Effect on the 119th Congress** (Voteview exports of 2026-10-03):

| | Senate | House |
|---|---|---|
| Saturation | 0.258 → 0.265 | 0.224 → 0.224 |
| Mean change in position congruence | 2.85 | 2.68 |
| Mean change in Constituent Alignment | 0.86 | 0.80 |

Most of that is every full record now counting at about 0.9, a tenth of
the way nearer 50. The largest changes are thin records:

- Two recently sworn-in representatives with no count move from 23.3 and
  31.1 to 50 on the component.
- A representative with 77 votes moves from 93.2 to 73.6.
- Members with 116–182 votes move 6 to 16 points.
- Members scored from 1 to 39 votes or a placeholder, all since departed,
  move by up to 50 points.

Separately, House positions refresh again now that the gate is fixed.
