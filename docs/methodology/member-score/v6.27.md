# Member score v6.27

## Constituent Alignment: a roll-call position from few votes is pulled toward neutral

**Before (v6.26).** Position congruence (30% of Constituent Alignment)
compares a member's congress-specific Nokken-Poole position with what a
same-party member of a seat with that lean typically holds. Every member's
position counted in full, however many roll calls it was estimated from,
and every member entered the per-party fits and the saturation scale the
score is read against.

**What went wrong.** A congress-specific position estimated from a few roll
calls is mostly noise. Over every member of Congresses 101–118, the gap
between a member's Nokken-Poole position and their career DW-NOMINATE
position is a median 0.03–0.04 for members with more than 75 votes in the
congress. For members with 26–50 votes it is 0.17, and the worst tenth of
members with 25 or fewer are 0.50 apart. At 20 votes the sampling noise
alone is worth about ±25 points on this component. Members sworn in late,
members who left early, and anyone early in a Congress were scored on these
estimates. Voteview also publishes a position for a newly sworn-in member
before it scales them, and those positions counted in full too.

**Now.**

- **The vote count a position needs is measured.** Squared gap = real
  congress-to-congress movement + noise that falls with the vote count
  (`k / votes`), fitted over 9,821 member-congresses. The noise equals the
  real movement at **81 votes**. `scripts/calibrate_position_confidence.py`
  fits this from Voteview's member files and writes
  `app/data/position_confidence.json`, which the scorer reads.
- **A thin record is shrunk toward 50** by `min(votes / 81, 1)`, the count
  confidence the rest of the score uses for sparse data. 50 is where a
  same-party member of the seat is expected to sit.
- **Only full records set the norm.** The per-party fits and the saturation
  scale are taken over members with at least 81 votes.
- **A position with no count reads as 0 votes**: Voteview hasn't scaled
  that member yet.
- **Early in a Congress**, until each party has 20 members with full
  records, the ingest keeps the previous good data, as for any other failed
  ingestion gate.

**What was tested and kept.** The same study tested the two other ways this
component differs from the vote part. Neither changed:

- **50 at the expectation, not 100.** Across House general elections
  1994–2010 and Senate general elections 1990–2024, the linear shape
  predicted the incumbent's vote share (t=5.2 and t=2.5). Peaking it at the
  expectation, as the vote part does, predicted nothing (t=0.5, t=−0.7).
  Sitting nearer the seat's center earns credit with voters, so it scores
  above 50. A typical member scoring about 50 here is expected.
- **One scale for both parties.** One scale per party predicts elections
  equally well, and the test of which one voters respond to was
  inconclusive. The pooled scale stays. Party averages are equal under both
  (over every Senate since 1989, 50.5 D and 51.6 R), but Republican
  positions spread more widely around their expectation, so more
  Republicans sit at 0 and at 100.

The study is `docs/research/constituent-alignment.md`, section 14
(reproduce with `scripts/research_constituent_alignment.py`).

**Effect on the 119th Congress** (Voteview data of 2026-10-03):

| | Senate | House |
|---|---|---|
| Saturation scale | 0.258 → 0.269 | 0.224 → 0.223 |
| Mean change in position congruence | 1.7 | 1.1 |
| Mean change in Constituent Alignment | 0.5 | 0.3 |

Four voting members still serving have thin records:

- Two recently sworn-in representatives have no count yet. They move from
  23.3 and 31.1 to 50 on the component, which is +8.0 and +5.7 on
  Constituent Alignment.
- A representative with 77 votes moves from 93.2 to 91.3.
- A senator with 53 votes moves from 55.0 to 54.9.
