# Member score v6.14

## Constituent Alignment: breaking far past the seat's expectation lowers the score

**Before.** The seat-relative vote component (70% of Constituent Alignment,
100% without ideal-point data) rose with a member's break rate above what
same-party members in similarly-leaning seats show. It saturated at 100 at the
chamber's 90th-percentile deviation and stayed there however much further the
member broke.

**Now.** It still peaks at 100 at that deviation. Past it, the score falls at
the same rate it rose: 50 at twice the saturation deviation, 0 at three times
(`OVER_BREAK_DECLINE = 1.0`, `_seat_relative_vote_score`). Scores for loyalty
below the expectation are unchanged.

**Why.** The question came from Sen. John Fetterman scoring 100 while breaking
with his party on 22.2% of party-labeled votes, against an expected 6.6%. The
member was elected by a seat and under a party label (Fenno 1978's concentric
constituencies), so both audiences were tested
(docs/research/constituent-alignment.md, section 8):

- **The whole seat (Senate general elections 1990–2024, N=461).** Crossing
  is rewarded up to saturation (+2.28 pts of vote share per SD, t=1.9) and
  flat past it (+0.19, t=0.2).
- **The member's own party (House primaries 1990–2010, N=3,867).** Among
  challenged incumbents, primary vote share falls past saturation (−3.0 pts
  per SD, t=−2.0; squared term −0.36, t=−2.3).
- **The literature.** It finds the same split: general electorates reward
  independence, while co-partisans and primary voters reward loyalty
  (Pyeatt 2015; Anderson, Butler & Harbridge-Yong 2020). No study supports
  treating ever-more defection as better representation.

**What is measured and what is chosen.** The turning point is the
saturation deviation the chamber already measures every run
(`compute_constituent_reference`). The decline rate is a design weight, not a
fitted one. Mirroring the rise was chosen because the two measured slopes are
of similar size with opposite signs (+2.3 rising, −3.0 falling), which gives
no support for an asymmetric shape. No published study estimates where "too
much" begins.

**Ground-truth gate.** The derived consistency gate's Constituent
Alignment check used to require the score to rise with the raw break rate.
It now ranks members by their weighted break rate relative to their seat's
expectation, folded at the saturation deviation
(`ground_truth.seat_relative_break_position`). The fold mirrors the score's
shape but not its decline rate, so a scorer that stopped turning down would
still be caught. The heaviest breakers therefore stay in one whole-chamber
check. A separate falling-side check would have had too few members to run in
the Senate, where only a handful sit past saturation.

Two further changes to the gate:

- A new integrity probe fails when more than twice `1 − SATURATION_QUANTILE`
  of members sit past saturation. That would mean the Constituent Alignment
  reference and the stored votes disagree.
- The gate now judges members against the reference the run actually scored
  with. The pipelines pass it in, and the persisted file is only the
  fallback.

`scripts/rescore.py` builds its gate inputs through the same helper
(`ground_truth.constituent_metrics`).
