# Member score v6.32

Two corrections found by the 2026-10-08 data audit.

## The candidate's own money is not outside money

Top-donor concentration, part of Funding Independence, is the top ten
outside donors' share of all outside money. Its stated definition takes the
candidate's own money out of that pool, but only the itemized donor rows
the classifier typed as self-funded were taken out. A candidate's loans to
the campaign and the candidate's own contributions, which the FEC reports as
separate totals, stayed in it as if a donor had given them.

Measured on the stored FEC totals: 95 sitting members have money from the
candidate in their election window; for 29 it is more than 10% of the
shares' base, and for one it is 95%. One campaign funded 85% by its
candidate's loans read its top ten donors as 1.3% of outside money.

Now the candidate's loans and contributions (FEC totals) are taken out of
the pool, and shown in the funding breakdown as their own category,
"Candidate's own money", instead of "Other sources". They stay in the base
the PAC and small-donor shares are taken over, as before: money a campaign
takes from its own candidate is money it didn't need from PACs. The category
is never an industry. These members' top-donor concentration rises and that
part of Funding Independence falls; "Measured effect" below has the
numbers.

## A vetoed bill is not a law

Congress.gov gives "Vetoed by President." the same action code (E30000) as
"Signed by President.", and the code alone decided the stage. Two bills
vetoed in December 2025, whose overrides failed, were stored as enacted:
they counted toward their sponsors' Legislative Effectiveness and were
listed under "Became law". The President action's own text now decides
signed or vetoed. Of the presidential action codes in the stored action
histories, E30000 was the only one that carried both (118 signings, 2
vetoes).

## Measured effect

Measured 2026-10-09 by replaying both member pipelines offline on a copy
of the production database taken after that night's run, every request
answered from the API responses that run cached: once with the code just
before this release and once with this release's, on the same responses
and the same learning store (the method of v6.31's "Measured effect").
The two parts were separated by swapping each back to the earlier
replay's inputs; together they reproduce the whole difference.

| Dimension | Senate (100) | House (433) |
|---|---|---|
| Funding Independence | 30 moved (23 up, 7 down), −12 to +1 | 174 moved (147 up, 27 down), −22 to +1 |
| Constituent Alignment | none | none |
| Legislative Effectiveness | none | 152 moved (142 up, 10 down), −23 to +2 |
| Funding Diversity (informational) | 8 moved, all down, −11 to −1 | 37 moved, all down, −15 to −1 |
| Overall | 30 moved, −4.0 to +0.3 | 257 moved, −7.8 to +1.0 |

Of the 533 members, 287 moved overall: 8 by 3 points or more, 5 by 5 or
more, none by 10. The mean overall score rose 0.05.

- **The candidate's own money.** 97 members have it in their window in
  the replayed data (19 senators, 78 representatives), 31 of them over 10%
  of the base. Funding Independence fell for 34 of them (7 senators, 27
  representatives): the largest falls were 22, 19, 16, three of 12 and 10
  points, and 18 members fell by 4 or more. Another 46 were unchanged and
  17 rose a point. Members without candidate money moved only through the
  chamber's concentration reference, measured on the corrected pools: 153
  rose a point.
  Funding Diversity fell for 45 members, all with candidate money, by 1 to
  15 points (median 4): the money moved out of "Other sources", which
  source breadth counts as neutral, into its own category, which counts
  with the opaque remainder.
- **Vetoed bills.** The two sponsors lost a law each: Legislative
  Effectiveness −22 and −23. Through the chamber's per-run reference, 142
  other representatives rose by 1 (one by 2) and 8 fell by 1. Both
  sponsors are representatives; no senator's score moved.
