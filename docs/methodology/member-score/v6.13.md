## Legislative Effectiveness: same-status benchmark

Production verification of this version (the House scored with main's code and
this version's) showed the gap between the parties' mean LE widening to 18.5
points: D 62.8, R 44.3. Every House Democrat is in the minority, so that
was a status effect.

The cause was the status adjustment. Expected credit was the chamber median ×
(the member's status advancement rate ÷ the chamber-average rate), with the
advancement rates from `_measure_advancement_rates`. That scales *all* credit by
an advancement-rate ratio, but most credit is introduction credit, which status
does not change. [`backend/scripts/research_les_status_benchmark.py`](../../../backend/scripts/research_les_status_benchmark.py)
simulates a House whose sponsors differ only in status, each performing exactly
at their status's rate:

| Benchmark | Majority median / mean | Minority median / mean |
|---|---|---|
| Advancement-rate tilt (before) | 7.5 / 16.8 | 97.0 / 86.2 |
| Same-status median (now) | 50.0 / 47.6 | 50.0 / 52.8 |

The two statuses' median credit differs by about 12% (95 vs 85). The tilt
assumed a factor of 2.7.

Expected credit is now the median credit of members of the same status, measured
each run (`compute_les_reference`'s `status_median`), which is Volden & Wiseman's
own benchmark. The tilt remains only as the fallback when either status has
fewer than 15 measurable members.
