# Member score v6.28

## House procedural votes no longer count as party loyalty anywhere

Whether a member voted with their party is meant to come only from how the
parties actually split on the roll call (`partySplit`, set by
`stamp_roll_call_outcome`). Housekeeping questions, the motions that run the
chamber rather than decide a bill, get no split on purpose (v6.19). Two
places in the House missed that.

### 1. House recent votes on the scorecard

**Before.** The House built the recent votes it stores for each member in a
loop of its own (`house_pipeline.py`) that had drifted from the shared code.
It decided `votedWithParty`, and the majority leader's reconsider-switch
exemption, from `partyLeaning`: a label `refine_with_vote_data` sets from the
split on every question, housekeeping included. Where a roll call gave no
usable split, `partyLeaning` would also have kept the bill's content label,
so content could decide a break, which AGENTS.md §4 rules out.

**What it affected.** Not Constituent Alignment's break rate, which since
v6.20 reads each member's party-line record over the whole Congress
(`party_line_record.py`) whenever that record exists; the stored flags reach
the score only in its fallback, a chamber with no stored roll calls or a
member none of them holds. They do drive what the scorecard shows: each
vote's with- or against-party badge, the "against party" filter and its
count, and key-vote selection (`cross_reference.select_key_votes` favours
votes against the party). The reconsider-switch flag also feeds partisan
depth.

Measured on the House's latest 120 roll calls of 2026 (rolls 195 to 314,
the window the House run reads;
`scripts/research_house_housekeeping_loyalty.py 2026`, 2026-10-04):

- 19 of the 120 are housekeeping questions: 10 motions to recommit, 7
  votes ordering the previous question, 1 motion to commit and 1 motion to
  table. The old rule counted 7,956 member-votes on them as with the party
  and 26 as against.
- No other roll call in the window lacked a split, so the content fallback
  did not fire there.
- The mean recent-vote break rate shown on profiles rises from 2.20% to
  2.79% for Republicans (220 members) and from 2.78% to 3.51% for Democrats
  (216); the largest single change is 12.0 points.

**Now.** Every stored vote, key or recent, in both chambers, is built by one
function, `normalize_votes.member_vote_entry`, which reads loyalty from
`partySplit` only. House recent votes also gain the roll call's date and
reference, which the Senate's already carried, so they sort by date with the
key votes and link to the roll call's record. A majority leader's switch on
a House housekeeping question is no longer flagged, as in the Senate; that
changes nothing, since partisan depth no longer reads housekeeping votes at
all (part 4).

### 2. The House's motion to commit

**Before.** The housekeeping list matched the motion to recommit but not the
House's motion to commit, the same minority motion for a bill no committee
reported (House Rule XIX). It counted in the party-line record, so it did
reach Constituent Alignment.

**Now.** It is housekeeping in the House (`is_housekeeping(question,
chamber)`). The Senate's motion to commit carries instructions that amend
the bill, so it is a vote on substance and still counts there.

**Effect.** In the whole 119th Congress the House held 4 motions to commit
(`--commit 119`), all party-line, and no member broke with their party on
any of them. Leaving them out drops at most 4 of a member's party-line votes
from the denominator of their break rate and changes no break, so
Constituent Alignment moves by a fraction of a point at most, and the
per-party reference (`compute_constituent_reference`, re-measured each run)
moves with it. The trend chart marks the version change regardless.

### 3. Party positions in the House run

The party positions partisan depth compares bills with start from each
party's platform and are blended with the bills earlier runs labelled for
that party (`party_platform._build_data_centroids`). Only the Senate run
built them, from Senate votes. The House classified against whatever its
process last cached: the Senate's centroids in the nightly chain, the bare
seeds for a House run triggered after a restart. Both runs now rebuild them
at start, from both chambers' labelled votes. This moves House partisan
depth, which is not part of the score.

### 4. A housekeeping roll call is not a vote on its bill

A motion to recommit or commit carries its bill's title, so it was
classified as the bill: it got the bill's policy areas, with the bill's own
per-area party labels, and a party label (`partyLeaning`) from its own
split, which runs opposite to the bill's (the minority moves to recommit).
In partisan depth a Yea on the motion counted toward the bill's party in the
bill's areas, the opposite of what the minority's motion means. And since
the bill's passage vote carries the same title with the other party label,
the party-position centroids, which read the stored `partyLeaning`, saw the
same text under both parties: on the House's latest 120 roll calls of 2026,
10 bills were labelled one party through their recommit or commit vote and
the other through passage. That only reached the House's centroids with
part 3, but partisan depth in both chambers had it.

`stamp_roll_call_outcome` now marks a housekeeping roll call procedural (no
policy areas, no party label), both pipelines skip the split refinement for
it, and a sponsored bill's badge is never refined from one. Rows stored
before this release still carry the bill's area and the split's label, so
the centroids also leave out any stored vote whose roll call's question is
housekeeping (`_build_data_centroids`, by the stored `roll_call`). Partisan
depth and the centroids no longer read these votes; no score does. Such
motions also no longer appear among the featured key votes or the
donor-industry vote matches, which skip procedural votes.

### Also removed

`record_sponsor_alignment` wrote a party label for each bill from its
sponsor's party into the learning store, and nothing read it. The write and
the comments describing sponsor-party refinement are gone.
