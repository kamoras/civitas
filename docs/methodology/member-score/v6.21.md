# Member score v6.21

## Funding: political committees are political money, by the FEC's registration

**Before (v6.20).** Each contributing committee's industry came from the
name classifier (`donor_classifier_ai` / `industry_classifier`), the same as
any organization's. The committee's FEC registration was fetched, but only
its type code (Q/N) was used, for the PAC per-election cap.

**What went wrong.** Committee names are not descriptions. Matched against
the FEC committee master files for 2020–2026, the September 2026 Senate's
top-donor lists contained political committees counted as industries for
57 of 101 senators:

| Committee (FEC type / designation) | Filed under | Senators | Total |
|---|---|---|---|
| NRSC (Y party / U) | GUNS | 19 | $918,700 |
| Pineapple PAC (Q / D leadership) | LOBBYISTS | 11 | $70,000 |
| Giddy Up PAC (Q / D) | LOBBYISTS | 12 | $70,000 |
| Making A Responsible Stand For Households In America PAC (Q / D) | GUNS | 8 | $70,000 |
| Velvet Hammer PAC (Q / D) | LABOR_UNIONS | 11 | $65,000 |

The NRSC was also the named headline of Lindsey Graham's GUNS donor-vote
connection.

**Now.** `fetch/fec.py` loads the FEC's bulk committee master (`cm{yy}.zip`,
one ~2.5 MB file per cycle, weekly cache) for the cycles a funding window
spans, and `normalize_finance` assigns industry `POLITICAL` to any committee
the FEC registers as:

- type **H, S, P** (candidate committees) or **X, Y, Z** (party committees);
- designation **A** (authorized by a candidate), **P** (principal campaign
  committee), **J** (joint fundraiser) or **D** (leadership PAC).

These are the FEC's own codes (data dictionary, "Committee type" and
"Committee designation"); no name is read. That is tier 1 of the
classification strategy, ahead of the embedding classifier, which still
decides every other committee. `POLITICAL` is one of `NON_INDUSTRY_CODES`, so
this money leaves industry concentration (HHI) and the donor-vote gate, and
stays in the funding totals.

The files cover 2016 onward (a senator's most recent completed election
can be six years back, and its window spans the three cycles before it).
For a committee missing from them the per-committee API is asked, and its
type and designation feed the same rule, so a failed bulk download slows the
run rather than switching the rule off.

The same master file supplies a PAC's type for the per-election cap and its
connected organization, used for lobbying lookups (below). That column is a
sponsor only for a separate segregated fund, the committees the FEC gives an
interest-group category (`ORG_TP`); elsewhere it holds joint-fundraising
partners ("TAKE BACK THE HOUSE 2022") or the form's "NONE" (28,595 rows of
the 2020–2026 files), so it is used only there, and not when it repeats the
committee's own name (157 of cm26's 2,067 sponsored funds). A sponsor that
is itself another PAC (a state bankers' PAC naming the American Bankers
Association's) is followed to that PAC's sponsor, unless the name belongs to
a committee the organization files under itself (FEC types C, E and I).
Chains are resolved over every cycle's file together, since a sponsor can cite
a PAC by a name it has since changed; measured over the 2020–2026 files, 41 of
cm26's sponsors resolve to an organization and 17 to none. A committee last
registered before the registration citing it is not followed. A committee with
no separate sponsor is searched under its own name, where the registry lists
no one, so a miss there is recorded as unknown rather than as no lobbying.

**Effect.** Recomputed from each live senator's industry breakdown with the
matched amounts moved to `POLITICAL`: Funding Independence changes by
−1.7 points at most (Bill Hagerty −1.69, Mike Lee −1.65, Chuck Grassley −1.64,
Chris Van Hollen −1.45), where political money had made industry funding
look more spread out, and by under 0.2 points for everyone else (median 0).
The component is small because most senators' classified industry share
already sits near the neutral blend (`_industry_concentration`'s relevance
weight). The NRSC can no longer head Graham's GUNS connection; in the live
breakdown GUNS is 2% of his classified industry money without it, under the
25% gate, so the connection should not reappear.

## Unscored changes in the same release

Donor-vote connections are informational, but these changes ship with this
version because they share its data:

- **Registered lobbying spend works again.** The registry moved from
  lda.senate.gov to lda.gov; the old host answers 301, httpx doesn't follow
  redirects by default, and every lookup had failed and been shown as $0
  (all 21 live Senate matches, including Goldman Sachs and BlackRock). A
  failed lookup is now recorded as unknown (`lobbying_checked = false`), and
  a run in which every lookup fails sends an ops alert.
- **Filings are shown with their client.** The registry's client-name
  search is loose ("APPLE" also returns Appleton International Airport and
  the US Apple Association), so a filing counts only when its client's name
  begins with the donor's (`lda.is_same_client`). That still admits a
  separate company sharing the name (an independent Coca-Cola bottler)
  alongside real subsidiaries (JPMorgan Chase Holdings), which no name rule
  separates, so spend is broken down by client name and each linked bill
  names the client its filing was for.
- **A PAC is looked up under its sponsor's name.** The registry lists
  "JPMORGAN CHASE HOLDINGS LLC", which a search for "JP MORGAN CHASE FEDERAL
  PAC" never finds; the FEC's connected-organization field gives the name
  that does.
- **Bills named in filings under the donor's name.** An LDA report lists the
  legislation lobbied on in free text. `analyze/lobbying_records.py` finds
  the bill numbers and keeps one only when the filer's words name that bill
  in the current congress, which three checks decide: its titles fit the
  words, no other bill of the congress fits them better, and the previous
  congress's bill with the same number doesn't either. Calibration on 2025
  filings by 48 large clients, and the cases that set each rule, are in the
  module docstring and reproduced by
  `backend/scripts/calibrate_lda_bill_match.py`. A filing says the
  organization lobbied on a bill, not for or against it, so nothing here
  says whether a vote went the donor's way.
