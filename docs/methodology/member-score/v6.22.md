# Member score v6.22

## Funding Independence: PAC share against campaigns of the same size

**Before (v6.21).** The PAC-dependency component (20/53 of Funding
Independence) scored a member's PAC share of contributions against their
chamber's median share, then multiplied it by a PAC-cap utilization factor:
how close each contributing PAC with a known committee type came to its
per-election limit ($5,000 multicandidate, $3,500 otherwise), from ×1.0 at
none to ×0.5 at the limit. Without committee-type data, a factor from the
member's PAC dollars against twice the chamber median stood in.

**What went wrong.** Two things, measured on the live breakdowns of
2026-09-28 (99 senators and 432 representatives currently serving):

1. **Share measured campaign size.** PAC checks are capped by law and
   individual money is not, so a larger campaign dilutes the same PAC
   dollars to a smaller share. In the Senate, PAC dollars barely grow with
   campaign size at all:

   | Chamber | log(PAC share) on log(contributions), slope | r | n |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | Senate | −1.03 | −0.69 | 91 |
   | House | −0.48 | −0.42 | 390 |

   (fit over members inside the chamber's 5th–95th percentile of campaign
   size with any PAC money). Funding Independence itself correlated with
   log campaign size at r = +0.58 in the Senate and +0.15 in the House: a
   senator from a large state scored as independent for having a large
   campaign. The About page disclosed this as "Fundraising scale still
   matters".

2. **The factor meant to correct it measured something else.** It
   averaged how close each contributing PAC came to one election's cap,
   which describes how hard each PAC gave, not how much the campaign
   depended on PACs: a member with a small PAC share from a few PACs that
   each gave the limit was halved. And the donor totals it read span the
   whole election period, a primary and a general, where a PAC can give the
   limit in each, so a PAC giving the legal maximum for the period read as
   twice the cap and was clipped to "maxed out". Across the chambers the
   factor's quartiles were ×0.57 / ×0.75 / ×0.84 (Senate) and ×0.54 /
   ×0.72 / ×0.84 (House). The component's own description ("the chamber's
   median member lands at 50") was therefore not true: the median member's
   PAC component was 35.3 (Senate) and 32.7 (House).

**Now.** `compute_funding_reference` fits, per chamber and every run, the
PAC share campaigns of each size typically take (`_pac_size_fit`):
log(PAC share) linear in log(contributions) by least squares, with the
intercept moved by the median residual so a member at the fit is the
typical member of that size. The component scores the member's share
against the fitted share at their size exactly as it scored against the
median before: at the typical share it is 50, with no PAC money 100, at
twice the typical share 0. The fit is read only inside the 5th–95th
percentile of campaign size it was fitted on: a campaign smaller or larger
than that is compared with the edge of the range, not an extrapolation.
Two House members whose records show only $18,551 and $68,266, all of it
from PACs (records that are plainly incomplete), would otherwise have been
compared with an expected share above 100% (367% and 195%) and scored 86
and 74; against the
range's smallest typical campaigns ($660,762, 65% PAC) their component is
23, and the breakdown names that size. The utilization and PAC-dollar factors are removed. Before
a chamber has a fit (fewer than 30 funded members, or a fresh deployment
without one), its median share is the reference, as before.

Committee type (Q/N) is still read from the FEC committee master and stored
on each donor, now for every committee contributor, not only rows the FEC
lists as entity type `COM`; nothing scores with it.

The ground-truth gate gains a second raw-FEC rank check: Funding
Independence must fall with a member's PAC dollars as well as with their
PAC share.

**Effect.** Recomputed with the new code from the live funding of the same
members (baseline: production's current scores):

| | Senate | House |
|---|---|---|
| FI vs log campaign size, r | +0.58 → +0.05 | +0.15 → −0.06 |
| Mean FI | 46.5 → 51.4 | 50.4 → 55.0 |
| Mean FI, Democrats | 53.3 → 55.5 | 50.7 → 55.6 |
| Mean FI, Republicans | 40.1 → 47.3 | 50.0 → 54.4 |
| Median change | +4 | +4 |
| Members moving more than 10 points | 37 | 35 |

The level rises because the utilization factor no longer pulls every
member's PAC component down (see 2 above). The Senate party gap narrows
from 13.2 to 8.2 points: part of it was the scale effect, since larger
states' senators, who raise the most individual money, were scored as
independent for it. The rank order still follows the raw FEC figures the
ground-truth gate checks (Spearman with FI: PAC share −0.56 Senate / −0.74
House; PAC dollars −0.88 / −0.79; all p < 1e−8).

Largest moves: Senate up to +27 (Cindy Hyde-Smith; Pete Ricketts and Peter
Welch +25: small-state campaigns whose PAC dollars are typical for a
campaign their size) and down to −15 (Gary Peters; Catherine Cortez Masto
and Tim Scott −14: large campaigns taking more PAC money than campaigns
their size typically do); House +16 to −8. Each member's funding was
scored with their own state and district, and the baseline is the score
production shows today.

**Reproduce.** `backend/scripts/audit_pac_ratio.py` regenerates the bundled
`app/data/funding_reference.json` (including the size fit) from the live
API with the same `compute_funding_reference` the pipeline runs.
