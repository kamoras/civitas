# Member score v6.39

Legislative Leadership (a quarter of Legislative Effectiveness where
coalition attraction is measured) is a PageRank over the chamber's
cosponsorship network, each cosponsorship weighted by how far the bill got:
enacted, advanced (its latest action passed a chamber, was agreed to or
reported) or stalled. The weights were 1.0, 0.6 and 0.3 in both chambers.
They are now fitted, per chamber, and read from
`app/data/sponsorship_edge_weights.json`: the Senate's are 1.0, 0.1 and
0.05; the House keeps 1.0, 0.6 and 0.3.

## Why

The 1.0/0.6/0.3 had no source. The code said they were "calibrated 2026-07
against the live corpus, see PR description"; that description went with
the squashed history, and no script reproduced them. AGENTS.md section 3a
asks for a calibrated value to come from a script and a data file.

## Measured

`scripts/calibrate_sponsorship_edge_weights.py`, on GovInfo's Bill Status
files for the 113th to 118th Congresses (every bill type; the same
sponsor, cosponsor, law and latest-action fields the pipeline reads from
Congress.gov), run 2026-11-10.

What the weights should predict: a member whose leadership is high in one
Congress should be one whose legislation moves in the next. For each
chamber and each transition (113th to 114th, and so on), leadership is
computed on Congress t's bills with the pipeline's own matrix and PageRank
(`sponsorship_analysis.pagerank`), and ranked against the number of bills
and joint resolutions each member sponsors in t+1 that advance or become
law. Simple and concurrent resolutions are left out of that count, since
most "agreed to" ones are commemorative. The ranking is within party,
averaged by the party's size. Across the whole chamber the House's 117th
to 118th transition (a change of majority) came out negative for every
weight, because the count then mostly reads which party held the chamber.

A grid over advanced and stalled (steps of 0.05, stalled never above
advanced) was tested forward in time, as the weights are always applied to
a Congress the fit hasn't seen: each transition from the chamber's third
Congress on was predicted by the best weights on the earlier Congresses
alone, and compared with 1.0/0.6/0.3 on the same transition.

| | Forward gain (Spearman) | SE | Best on all transitions | Mean, fitted | Mean, 1.0/0.6/0.3 | Mean, flat |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Senate | +0.045 | 0.012 | 1.0 / 0.1 / 0.05 | 0.282 | 0.248 | 0.244 |
| House | -0.000 | 0.002 | 1.0 / 1.0 / 0.15 | 0.166 | 0.163 | 0.165 |

The Senate's fitted weights beat the old ones on each of the three held-out
transitions (0.298 against 0.272, 0.306 against 0.265, 0.276 against
0.209). The House's best is no better forward, so the House keeps its
weights. A chamber takes a fit only when its forward gain is more than one
standard error above zero.

One set of weights for both chambers was measured first: forward it gained
0.016 within party (SE 0.013), the Senate gaining 0.042 (SE 0.010) and the
House losing 0.010 (SE 0.004). That split is what moved the fit to one set
per chamber. The rule was chosen after seeing it; three forward
transitions per chamber are few, and the refit below decides again on each
new Congress.

## What moves

Only the Senate's leadership. On the 118th Congress's cosponsorship
network, the senators' rankings under the two sets of weights correlate
0.94 (rank); the median senator moves 3 places, 22 of 104 move 10 or more,
and the largest move is 53. On the 117th: 0.95, 4 places, 18 of 101, 44.
Leadership is shrunk for short tenure and is one part of Legislative
Effectiveness, so scores move less than these ranks.

## Refit

`data_refresh` reruns the script once a Congress has ended past the newest
one the fit scored, and installs it only under the acceptance rule
(`held_out_beats_in_use`) on the forward comparison; an installed refit
moves the scoring version by itself.
