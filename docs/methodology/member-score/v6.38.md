# Member score v6.38

A roll call is a party-line vote when a majority of voting Republicans opposed
a majority of voting Democrats (`normalize_votes.compute_party_vote_split`).
It was at least 65% of one party voting Yea and at most 35% of the other.

## Why

Nothing gave 65/35 a source. The code called it Congressional Quarterly's
party unity vote, but CQ's definition is a majority against a majority: since
1956 CQ has scored members' party unity on the roll calls where a majority of
voting Democrats opposed a majority of voting Republicans. It is the
definition the congressional-parties literature counts party votes by (Rohde
1991, *Parties and Leaders in the Postreform House*, among many). The other
threshold with a pedigree is Lowell's (1902) "party vote", 90% of one party
against 90% of the other: far stricter. No source using 65/35, or two thirds
against one third, turned up.

## Measured

On the 119th Congress's stored roll calls and positions (production,
2026-10-22; 1,591 roll calls, 383,736 positions), leaving out housekeeping
questions (`is_housekeeping`). A member's break rate here is breaks over
party-line votes cast, per roll call. That is a simplification of the scored
record (`party_line_record`, which counts each measure once and only breaks
toward the other party), used to compare the rules with each other.

| | 65/35 | Majority (CQ) | Lowell 90/90 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Senate party-line roll calls (of 889) | 775 | 817 | 684 |
| House party-line roll calls (of 532) | 305 | 330 | 252 |
| Senate median break rate | 0.46% | 0.64% | 0.15% |
| House median break rate | 0.99% | 2.45% | 0.00% |

Against 65/35, member by member (members with 20 or more party-line votes):

| | Senate (100) | House (446) |
|---|---|---|
| Pearson correlation of break rates | 0.96 | 0.99 |
| Rank correlation | 0.96 | 0.89 |
| Median change in rank | 7 places | 35 places |
| Largest rise in break rate | 4.3 points | 8.5 points |

The roll calls that change label are the ones where a party split between
roughly 50% and 65%. Under the majority rule a vote with the other party on
such a roll call counts as a break; under 65/35 it counted for nothing.

## Effect on scores

Constituent Alignment scores each member's break rate against the rate the
seat predicts (`compute_constituent_reference`). That expectation is measured
in the same run, on the same rule, so the higher rates under the majority rule
move the reference with them. What moves a score is a member's rate relative
to their party's members in similar seats: the rank correlations above. The
first Senate and House runs after the change recompute every member's score
and the reference.

The scores were not replayed end to end before the change: that needs a full
member run.
