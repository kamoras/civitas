# Member score v6.16

## Constituent Alignment: highest at the seat's norm, measured in standard deviations

The full study is section 10 of
[`docs/research/constituent-alignment.md`](../../research/constituent-alignment.md);
every number is printed by `backend/scripts/research_constituent_alignment.py`.

**Before (v6.15).** The seat-relative vote component scored 50 at the break
rate same-party members of similarly-leaning seats show, rose to 100 one
chamber-wide percentage-point scale above it, and fell back to 0 at three.
Loyalty fell to 0 at four scales below.

**Why it changed.** On the live 119th Senate:

- A Democratic senator broke on 3.5% against an expected 7.1% and scored 41. The
  least-squares expectation was pulled up by a few heavy breakers, so the
  typical member sat below it: 33 of 47 Democrats and 46 of 53 Republicans
  scored under 50.
- A Republican senator broke on 5.5% against an expected 1.5% and scored 90. One
  point scale for the chamber (4.9 points) counted four extra points the same
  whether the seat's members break 1.5% or 7% of the time.

**Now.**

- **Gap in standard deviations per vote** (`seat_residual`): the Pearson
  residual (rate − p) / √(p(1 − p)), with p kept half a vote from 0 and 1.
- **Fractional-logit expectation** (`compute_constituent_reference`), fitted
  by Newton's method with step-halving. A straight line predicted 0% for the
  safest seats, and plain reweighted least squares diverged on the 118th
  Senate.
- **100 at the expectation**, falling linearly to 0 at 1.5 scales above and
  3 below (`CROSSING_ZERO_GAPS`, `LOYAL_ZERO_GAPS`, `_vote_shape`). A
  breakdown's "0 at X" is that multiple times the party's scale: in the
  September 2026 Senate, 1.5 × 0.37 ≈ 0.56 for Democrats and 1.5 × 0.33 ≈ 0.49
  for Republicans. The multiples are design weights; the research note
  (section 10, "Where a breakdown's '0 at' number comes from") gives what they
  do on the live Senate: 5 senators at 0, and no one near the loyal-side zero.
- **One scale per party**: each party's 90th-percentile |residual|. On a
  pooled scale the parties' averages differed by 7.9 points across the
  101st–119th Senates (22.3 at worst, the sign following whichever party was
  more unified); per party, 1.8, the same as v6.15.
- The reference stamp (`CONSTITUENT_REFERENCE_STATISTIC`) changed, so a
  persisted v6.15 reference is re-measured and stored scores rescored on
  first startup (`constituent_rescore.py`).
- `benchmark_validation.py` checks stored Constituent Alignment against a
  recomputation from Voteview (vote shape plus position congruence at the
  scorer's weights). The old check that the score falls as Nokken-Poole
  extremity rises no longer holds by design: the heaviest breakers are often
  the most center-ward members, and now score low.

**Evidence, and the choice it forces.** Same members and outcomes as the
earlier sections:

| Outcome | v6.15 | v6.16 |
|---|---|---|
| House primaries 1990–2010, contested, primary share | −0.015/pt (t=−0.5) | +0.053/pt (t=2.4) |
| Senate generals 1990–2024, vote share | +0.035/pt (t=1.2) | −0.039/pt (t=−2.6) |
| House 2004 general, vote share | +0.088/pt (t=4.4) | −0.032/pt (t=−2.5) |

The member's own party's voters reward the new shape. The whole electorate
rewards breaking more than the seat's norm, and does not punish even heavy
breakers. v6.16 follows the first: the score measures whether a member does
what they were elected to do, under a party label as well as by a seat, not
what maximizes their general-election vote share. The About page says so.

**Effect on the live Senate** (site data, 2026-09):

| Senator | Break rate | Expected | v6.15 | v6.16 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D, at the norm | 5.4% | 5.6% | 48 | 99 |
| D, more loyal | 3.5% | 9.1% | 41 | 83 |
| R, never broke | 0.0% | 2.4% | 43 | 84 |
| D, above the norm | 11.0% | 6.7% | 99 | 69 |
| R, far above the norm | 5.5% | 1.3% | 90 | 26 |
| D, far above the norm | 16.3% | 5.6% | 42 | 17 |
| D, furthest above | 22.2% | 7.0% | 0 | 0 |

The expected rates are v6.16's logit fit on the same records, so they differ
from v6.15's. The majority leader is not in the table: every one of the
leader's breaks was the procedural switch vote, which this release stops
counting (see below), so that rate is not comparable until the next run.

**Thin records.** With fewer than 20 party-labeled votes the score is pulled
toward what a typical full-confidence member of the same party scores (the
reference's per-party `typical`, the median vote shape, measured each run),
not toward 50. Once matching the norm scores 100, 50 sits below nearly every
member, and shrinking toward it ranked special-election and appointed
newcomers under their colleagues for having few votes. The bundled prior,
which has no population to measure, still uses 50.

**Fits that don't exist.** When a party's rates are separable (every
opposed-seat member at zero breaks, common early in a Congress), the logit
has no finite coefficient. The fit is then refitted without the kink, and if
that has none either, no reference is measured that run and the last
persisted one is used (`_MAX_LOGIT_COEFFICIENT`).

**Majority-leader switch votes.** A majority leader votes with the prevailing
side against their own party, a Nay on a motion about to fail or a Yea on one
carrying over their party's opposition, so that they can move to reconsider
it (Senate Rule XIII; House Rule XIX cl. 2). These no longer count as breaks,
within the member's tenure as majority leader and only when the chamber
recorded the outcome.
