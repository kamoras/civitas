# Member score v6.30

Two corrections to the funding inputs: which election's money is read, and
which industry a PAC's money is filed under.

## 1. Funding is read from the election that seated the member

Funding Independence and Funding Diversity cover the member's most recent
completed election (AGENTS.md §6). Two gaps let ten House members be scored
on a different race.

### 1a. The chamber never reached the window

`select_recent_elections` drops a completed election too old to have won the
seat held now (`seat_winning_floor`: two years back for the House, six for
the Senate). It reads the chamber from the candidate record. A member matched
through the bioguide-to-FEC crosswalk gets a record holding only the id, so
the bound was off, and "most recent completed election" reached back to
whatever earlier race the id carried: a losing run from 2020, 2016, 2014,
2008, and in one case 2002. Both chamber runs now pass the chamber with the
record.

### 1b. FEC's candidate totals can omit the race that won the seat

FEC's candidate totals (`/candidate/{id}/totals/`) attribute money to an
election through the candidate's election-year link. For eight members seated
by the 2024 general, that link is missing: their totals list the 2026
campaign and an older run, but not 2024. Some of them show no 2024 election
anywhere in FEC's candidate profile either. The committee that ran the 2024
race still reports the money under its 2024 cycle
(`/committee/{id}/totals/`).

With only the bound from part 1, these members would be scored on their
unfinished 2026 campaign. Instead, for a member sworn in when the Congress
convened (the House Clerk's date, January 3 of its first year), the House
run adds the missing election from their principal committees' totals for
that cycle (`fec.with_seat_election`). These are FEC's own figures for those
committees; nothing is estimated. A member seated by a special election is
left on the campaign FEC records for them, because their seat-winning race
is that one.

### Effect (data of 2026-10-08)

| | members |
|---|---|
| House members whose window changes | 10 |
| from an old run to the 2024 race | 8 |
| from an old run to their 2026 special-election campaign | 2 |
| Senators | 0 (checked: every senator's window is unchanged with the bound) |

The changed windows run from $67,554 raised (a 2002 race) to $735,661 (2024),
and from $0.71M (the unfinished 2026 campaign) to $6.48M (2024). Funding
Independence moves for those members alone.

## 2. A PAC's industry comes from the records where they state it

Funding Diversity and the industry breakdown file each PAC's money under an
industry. That was read from the PAC's name by embedding similarity to a
prototype text per industry, and the stored labels were often wrong.

### Measured (donors stored 2026-10-08)

- 866 of 4,136 rows from PACs that labor organizations sponsor (21%) carried
  another industry. One union's PAC had 203 rows filed under GUNS.
- On corporate PACs whose sponsor is an SEC-registered issuer, the stored
  label agreed with the industry of the SIC code the SEC assigned the sponsor
  on 59.5% of rows (3,237 of 5,439).
- 304 donor names were filed under GUNS. Most were trade associations whose
  names share "National ... Association" with the "National Rifle
  Association" written into the GUNS prototype: auto dealers, trial lawyers,
  convenience stores, corn growers.

### What changed

**Records first** (`fec.structured_industry`). Before any reading of a name:
- a party, candidate, joint-fundraising or leadership committee is POLITICAL
  (unchanged);
- a PAC whose FEC registration names a labor organization as its sponsor
  (organization type `L`) is LABOR_UNIONS;
- a corporation's PAC whose sponsor is an SEC-registered issuer takes that
  issuer's SIC industry, the same decoding stock trades use. A sponsor
  matches an SEC title exactly once legal-form words ("Inc.",
  "Corporation", a leading "The") are dropped. Without that, exact names
  missed 45% of the matches.

**Prototypes without organization names in GUNS and REAL_ESTATE.** Measured
on the 1,861 distinct PAC donor names: GUNS fell from 97 names to 6, and 91
of the 160 changed labels left GUNS, nearly all for a better label or none
(kNN then decides). The Realtors' PAC stays REAL_ESTATE. Three real
gun-issue committees, the NRA's political fund and two gun-safety PACs, now
read POLITICAL or LOBBYISTS, one side each. Adding any of those names back
brought 81 to 119 names into GUNS, mostly wrong, so they stay out. About
five of the 39 REAL_ESTATE changes are worse (a rental-home REIT now reads
LOBBYISTS).

Removing organization names from every prototype was measured too
(`scripts/evaluate_industry_classifier.py`, 353 SEC-labelled corporate
PACs): no significant difference on companies (McNemar p = 0.42), and worse
on union names (p < 0.001), which the records now decide anyway. The other
prototypes are unchanged.

**Provenance.** A donor whose *type* came from FEC metadata had the
embedding's *industry* stored as source "fec" at confidence 1.0. Each label
is now stored under its own tier. Changing the classifier also changes the
analysis-code fingerprint, which clears the learning store, so the labels
kNN learned from start fresh.

### What this does not reach

Trade and membership associations still get their industry from their name,
since no record here states one, and the instrument cannot score them.
Subsidiaries of foreign or private parents (a carmaker's US arm, a railroad
owned by a holding company) aren't SEC titles and also fall to the name
classifier.

## Also in this release (no score effect)

Failed FEC requests now raise `FecUnavailable` instead of being cached as
`[]`. A member whose FEC data can't be read is skipped for the night, so
their stored record stays. Before, a dead request could store a member as
raising nothing.
