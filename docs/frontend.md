# Frontend notes

Moved from the README. AGENTS.md states the rules; this page keeps the longer description and the measurements behind them.

Page-by-page decisions about layout, type, phones and performance, each with what it measured.

## Layout, type and phones

**A missing record says where to go next.** A member, bill, issue, document or state address with nothing behind it is a real 404 (marked noindex), and its page offers the next step for that kind of record (`not-found.tsx` in each route, `components/notFound/RecordNotFound.tsx`): a member's address lists the politicians whose addresses are closest to it, one tap each (`GET /api/politicians/suggest`, difflib's similarity of the address to each id and to each word of it, from 0.75), then the directory; a bill's the bills list and the latest day in Congress; an issue's today's issues and the archive; a document's Explore search; a state ballot's or a state map's the ballot of the state the address still names (a full name like `/elections/states/ohio`, or a state that didn't redraw its House map), then the redrawn states and the elections map. A mistyped member address took five taps on a phone to reach the member (menu, directory, search box, type, result); it now takes one.

**Documents in boxes (2026-10).** Each result in the documents search (`/search?tab=documents`, its open comment periods, and the president's documents that `/executive/orders` opens) is a box: agency or chamber, type, date, member and citations over the title; where a comment period is open, its days left as the figure (the backend's `commentWindow`) and "Comment" as the command; for a search, the passage that matched; and the abstract, quoted and credited, behind "More" (it was printed on every card, 75 to 83 words at grade 15 to 18). Measured on an iPhone SE (375 px) with the density audit's method on a local `next build` against the same database snapshot, main against this change: the open comment periods went from 18.2 screens to 13.1 and their longest block from 75 words to 28, the president's documents from 12.6 screens to 9.6 (83 words to 28), and a search for "tariffs" from 12.0 to 11.8; the first result is where it was (929 px against 928).

**Long pages on a phone have a section bar.** Under 640px, a page that runs over six screens with three or more marked sections (a member's page, a bill, a roll call, a state ballot, `/court`, the About chapters) shows a 44px bar fixed under the header once its first section reaches it: the section being read on the left, `[ SECTIONS ▾ ]` on the right, which opens every section in a bottom sheet with its count ("Votes · 47 recorded", a score), the current one `aria-current="location"` (`components/shared/OnThisPage.tsx`). A page marks a heading with `onThisPage(label, count)` (`lib/onThisPage.ts`) and the bar reads them from the page, so sections that load later join it and it can't drift from the headings. A jump moves focus to the heading, with no smooth scrolling; the phone's `scroll-padding-top` grows by the bar's 47px while a page has one (2.4.11), and the header's live height is `--header-height` (`HeaderClearance`). Fixed rather than sticky: a sticky bar sticks only inside its parent, and a member page's sections sit in three. The sheet is the site's one dialog component (`components/shared/Modal.tsx`), a bottom sheet under 640px (at most 85% of the screen, the 3px ledger rule on top, a 44px `[ × CLOSE ]`, focus on its title, Escape or a tap outside closes it) and centered above; the share dialog uses it too. Reaching "Votes against party" on a member page went from about four screens of swiping to two taps; the last section of `/about/scores` from 30.7 screens.

**About pages open with one line (2026-10).** Each About chapter's standing line is one phone line ("Where every number comes from."), and its full introduction sits behind "More: the chapter's introduction" at the foot of "The short version" (`AboutPage` `intro`); the overview's sits behind the first principle's "More". `/about/references` is boxed: each work a compact record box (who and when as the title, the full entry as the line, its `#id` on the box, outlined on arrival) in a ruled grid under its first author's initial, with what a citation means behind the first box's "More". Measured on an iPhone SE (375 px) with the density audit's method, main against this change on a production build: the first box moved up on every chapter (the overview 1.15 to 0.96 screens, `/about/presidents-and-justices` 1.68 to 1.50, `/about/news` 2.46 to 2.36, `/about/scores` 2.49 to 2.42, `/about/elections` 2.51 to 2.45, `/about/data` 3.54 to 3.52), first-screen prose fell (the overview 22% to 0%, the chapters by 5 to 9 points), and the first reference moved from 0.66 to 0.49 screens; the references page grew on a phone from 11.7 screens to 14.6 (two columns from 40rem).

**A phone's first screen carries the point.** Under 640px a section's sub-pages (`SectionTabs`) are one 44px row naming the section and the page ("Congress ▸ Bills ▾"), a native `<details>` of the same links (not a navigating `<select>`, which would move a reader to another page while they look at the list, WCAG 3.2.2); the wrapped rows took 88-131px of the first screen (up to 250px on some pages). Pages with that row drop their breadcrumb and back link under 640px, since the row names where the page sits. A detail page's feed line (`FollowFeed`) reads "Follow · feeds" there, two 44px targets with each link's full name kept for screen readers, at the end of the hero (a bill's after its stage; a member's on the breadcrumb row). Explore's results page folds its standing line behind "More about this page" (`PageMasthead`'s `more`). Measured at 390px: a bill's "What it does" 687 → 530px, a roll call's tally 628 → 522px, the seat map 732 → 689px, Explore's first match 755 → 663px.

**One style per role.** Every piece of text takes one of thirteen roles in `src/lib/typeStyles.ts` (TYPE): the serif means a heading or a figure, the sans means read this. Sizes exist only as the scale's steps (44, 36, 24, 20, 18 and 16px for text, 56 and 30px for figures; 30, 28, 22, 18, 17, 15, 40 and 26px under 640px), so nothing a reader must read is under 15px on a phone; Tailwind's own sizes are switched off. Measured on 26 pages at 1280px (2026-10-22): family/size/weight combinations in the main column 43 → 13, font sizes 22 → 10, h1 looks 5 → 2, h2 13 → 4. `typeScale.test.ts` and `scripts/axe-routes.mjs` hold it. **Body 18px, small for captions only** (2026-10-22): small (then 14px) had become most of the main column's text, 95% of a member page, 84% of a state ballot, 69% of the homepage and 68% of the Action Center on production, because it carried running sentences as well as source lines; readability rises with size to about 18pt and 14 measured worse (Rello, Pielot & Marcos, CHI 2016). Body went from 16 to 18px (17 on a phone), small from 14 to 16 (15), dek and box titles from 18 to 20 (18), and a sentence read as prose (a box's line, a finding, a note, a disclosure's text) is body. Main-column text under 16px at 1280px, on the UI fixture's production build before and after: homepage 81% → 1%, a member page 69% → 0%, a state ballot 81% → 0%, the Action Center 42% → 0%; on a phone, where small is 15px, the share at the small step fell from 80% to 73%, 69% to 60%, 69% to 60% and 42% to 34%.

**Sentence case, not capitals (2026-10).** A reader said the capitals felt intimidating. Labels, kickers, buttons, nav items, tabs, tags and table headers are now sentence case with no wide letter-spacing, and text that was typed in capitals or raised with `.toUpperCase()` is written as words, so a screen reader no longer spells it out. The evidence (Tinker; Perea, Rosa & Marcet 2017; Arditi & Cho 2007; BDA and GOV.UK guidance) leaves one narrow use, a one-to-three-word section eyebrow, which goes through `EYEBROW` (`lib/typeStyles.ts`); the logotype keeps its capitals, and acronyms, identifiers and records quoted as printed keep theirs. `noAllCaps.test.ts` holds it (AGENTS.md, "Letter case").

**A phone's header is one row and gets out of the way.** Under 56rem (the MENU layout) the records band ("FILED … · COMPLETE") folds into a status square left of the wordmark (`RunStatusSquare` in `RecordsBand.tsx`): filled when every nightly run completed, hollow and red or amber otherwise, in a 44px button named with the whole status line, which a tap opens under the bar. The header goes from 86px to 51px (13.2% → 7.7% of an iPhone 14's screen; `--header-clearance` 3.75rem there). Past the first screen it hides while the reader scrolls down and comes back on any scroll up (`useHideOnScroll` in `Navbar.tsx`), never with reduced motion asked for, never while focus is in it (focus there shows it) and never with the menu or a sheet open; `data-header-hidden` on `<html>` sets `--header-shown-h` to 0, and the section bar and the Action Center's tab row (which read it, not the old literal `top-[82px]` that tucked 4px under an 86px header) move up to the top edge with it.

**"Your state" leads somewhere.** Picking a state in the header (this tab only, as before) now confirms it in the picker's polite live region with two next steps, "Ohio's members →" (`/congress/members?state=OH`) and "Ohio's ballot →", 44px each, with no timer while they show (`YourStatePicker`). the home page's members and ballot boxes lead with "Ohio's members →" and "Ohio's ballot →" (`StateJump`), `/elections` leads with "Your state: Ohio" and both links, and a roll call (and a bill's vote) lists the state's members and how each voted before any list is opened (`VoteMembers`). The phone menu is in phone order: the eight sections as 52px rows in the sans face, each section's pages behind its own ▸ (the current section's open), then the state, then Bluesky; it showed 33 caps links at one weight, the pages beside their sections. My members from the header: MENU, pick, "Ohio's members", 3 taps and a pick (5 taps and a pick before, with no confirmation).

**Standalone actions are 44px on a phone** (mobile spec P10): an issue's `[ CALL … ]`, `[ WRITE ↗ ]` and `[ WHAT TO SAY ]` (32px before), its action rows, "Read full story" and "Comment →", the share links, the homepage's voter links, the pagers (Previous/Next, numbered pages, the Action Center's day pager), the names on a roll call, and `/elections`'s state directory, which is two columns of named rows there (`TAP_TARGET` in `components/action/IssueEnrichment.tsx` for the issue's links). Links inside a sentence and the footer's directory keep their own height (24px, WCAG 2.5.8's minimum). Targets under 44px at 390px: a roll call 148 → 48, `/elections` 186 → 135, the Action Center 78 → 61, an issue 56 → 49 (most of what is left on each is the footer and links in running text).

**Server rendering:** `/action`, `/congress/members` and the other
search-param pages keep their view state in the address bar, and `useSearchParams()` has
no value during SSR — so React suspends and the server renders the nearest
`<Suspense>` fallback. Each of those pages supplies a real one
(`components/layout/PageFallback.tsx`: navbar, masthead, content skeleton), so
the frame arrives in the first paint. They previously wrapped the *entire* page
in `<Suspense fallback={null}>`, which made the server send a ~12KB body with no
chrome and nothing visible until ~653KB of JavaScript had parsed and hydrated.
The fallback carries the page's own masthead line at the page's own width
(`lede`, `width`), so the largest text in the first screen is server-sent:
/action, /explore and /politicians painted it at 1.9 s on a throttled phone
before (2026-10-14), 0.8 s after. Pages whose first screen depends on data
or on the query string render per request instead (`await connection()`, or
reading `searchParams`), with the data the first view needs read on the
server and handed to the client as its first state: `/elections` (the lean
map and phase, `elections/layout.tsx`), `/leaderboard` (the branch the URL
asks for and its field strip, `leaderboard/page.tsx`), `/congress/bills`
(the pipeline overview, `congress/bills/layout.tsx`) and `/compare` (a
linked pair's loading view keeps the masthead). Mobile LCP on those went
from 1.7–2.1 s to 0.8–0.86 s and leaderboard CLS from 0.09 to 0. Anything
the server could not read, the page still reads itself.

**Text boxes on a phone don't zoom the page.** iOS Safari zooms the whole layout when a control whose text is under 16px takes focus, and leaves it zoomed; every search box and filter on the site was 12-14px (11 of 12 measured at 390px, 2026-10-16). Under 640px every input, select and textarea is set at `max(16px, 1rem)` and at least 44px tall (`globals.css`, "Form controls on a phone"), without `maximum-scale=1`, which would stop a reader zooming on purpose. `scripts/axe-routes.mjs` fails a route whose controls compute under 16px or 44px at 320px.

**The Court page's sections lead with one line (2026-10).** On `/court`, "Where the justices sit" and "Who votes with whom" each open with one line, their method behind "More" (RecordBox's `More`, exported for a section that is a box around a chart), and "Methods and sources" is five boxes, each paragraph behind its "More". Measured on an iPhone SE (375 px) with the density audit's method on a local `next build` against the same database snapshot, main against this change: the longest block went from 88 words to 19 and the page from 11.6 screens to 10.3, with the Martin-Quinn line where it was (834 px).

## Same-origin photos

**Pages request nothing from other hosts.** Every photo (members' official photos, presidents' public-domain portraits, justices' Oyez photos, an Action Center issue's licensed news photo) is served from the site at `/photo/<kind>/<id>`: the API gives that path (`backend/app/photos.py`), and the frontend route `app/photo/[kind]/[id]/route.ts` fetches the picture server-side by kind and id (never a URL; the source comes from `GET /api/photo-sources/{kind}/{id}`). A member's photo is one of two official photos: the headshot on the member's Congress.gov record (its `depiction`, used only on Congress.gov's `/img/member/` path and only with the attribution Congress.gov gives it) or bioguide's portrait. Neither is always the head-and-shoulders shot (bioguide's is for some members a full-length painting; a few members' own depictions are full-figure or wide shots), so the pipeline measures the face in each with the YuNet face detector and keeps the depiction unless its face is small and bioguide's clearly larger (`backend/app/pipeline/fetch/member_photos.py`, nightly in the stock-trades run; thresholds refit daily from the stored face shares alone by `backend/scripts/calibrate_member_photos.py`, run by `app/data_refresh.py`: the ceiling under the headshot component of a mixture over every photo's face share, the margin past the noise of one picture measured twice; the hand-labelled comparison in `app/data/member_photo_calibration.json` is the held-out check under `precision_rule.accept`, and a refit takes effect on every stored choice the next night). The model is YuNet's `face_detection_yunet_2023mar.onnx` from the [OpenCV Zoo](https://github.com/opencv/opencv_zoo/tree/main/models/face_detection_yunet) (MIT License, Copyright (c) 2020 Shiqi Yu), vendored in `backend/app/data/` and run with `opencv-python-headless` in a child process of its own (`pipeline/fetch/face_detection.py`), so OpenCV is never loaded by the API or the pipeline process. The endpoint reads the stored choice (`bioguide_photo_source` in `backend/app/photos.py`). Each kind is fetched only from its own hosts, over https: www.congress.gov and bioguide.congress.gov for members, api.oyez.org, thumb.wikimedia.org, upload.wikimedia.org and ids.si.edu for presidents' portraits (the president pipeline reads Wikidata's image for each holder of the office by its series ordinal and keeps only a file Wikimedia Commons marks public domain; where that image isn't, a National Portrait Gallery object the Smithsonian marks CC0 whose one sitter is the president, from its open access API, stands in, on ids.si.edu; each with its author and license as the credit shown on the page; `pipeline/fetch/president_portraits.py`), and rollcall.com for issue photos (`PHOTO_SOURCE_HOSTS` in `backend/app/photos.py`), enforced by the pipeline, the endpoint, and the route on every redirect hop, since an issue photo's URL comes from an RSS item. nginx keeps each one a week (`photo_cache`), serves the last copy when a source is down, and rate-limits only cache misses, through the same loopback hop as the API: 10/s per client with a burst of 600, the first 60 immediate, so a cold directory page (543 portraits) is paced, never refused. A photo the source doesn't have is a 404, shown as initials (`components/Photo.tsx`). Pages show each photo scaled to its slot: `/photo/<kind>/<id>/<width>` is the same photo resized with sharp to one of five widths (96, 192, 400, 800, 1200; `PHOTO_WIDTHS` in `lib/photos.ts`), never up, as WebP; any other width is a 404, so nginx, which keys this cache on the path, holds at most those five copies of a photo besides the original. `Photo` gives every image a `srcset` of those widths and the slot's `sizes` (required, so a new photo can't ship without one), and the Action Center's lead issue photo and an issue page's photo are fetched first (`fetchpriority="high"`). Until 2026-10 an issue's 1620×1080, 364 KB photo was sent whole into a 341px slot on a phone, the slowest Largest Contentful Paint on the site (4.1 s on `/action`, 3.7 s on an issue page, at 375px with slow 4G and a 4× slower CPU). Fonts, scripts, styles and map data are self-hosted; `tests/test_same_origin_photos.py` and `frontend/src/lib/sameOriginImages.test.ts` fail on an absolute image URL on another host.

## Entered into the record (the homepage's front page)

The homepage opens with "Entered into the record", laid out as a newspaper front page from `GET /api/front-page` (`api/notable.py`), rendered on the server with the page (`components/home/FrontPage.tsx`). Top to bottom: a nameplate whose dateline is the Eastern date, Congress's state from the Record ("In session today", or "Congress is out" with when each chamber next sits, `congress_service.recess` and `next_meeting`) and the days to Election Day (`active_election()`), with an edition line naming the sources and linking the week; one lead story with a big figure; a second tier of two stories, each with a small figure; then three ruled columns, Congress, Agencies and In the news, each item dated with an inline figure where its data has one, each column ending in a "see more" link. On a phone it stacks lead, tier, then the columns. Every headline is a fixed template around stored figures or the record's own words (a law's title, a rule's title, an outlet's headline); no model writes any of it, and every figure (yeas and nays, a party's dot row, days left in a comment period, a count) is computed in the backend and only drawn by the page.

The candidates, all from stored rows:

- **Closest vote.** Of the latest week of business (the Monday-to-Sunday week holding the latest day either chamber took a record vote or passed, failed or confirmed anything, `congress_service.last_business_day`, the day `/congress` names when Congress is out), the simple-majority question with the smallest gap between yeas and nays ("Decided by 1 vote in the Senate, 49–50: Amendment Rejected."), newest on a tie; a tie the Vice President broke says so. A 3/5 or 2/3 question is left out, since how close it came depends on its threshold, and so is a housekeeping question (`normalize_votes.is_housekeeping`). Drawn as a yea/nay bar; "How your senators voted" opens the roll call's member list, where the state chosen in the header comes first.
- **Lone break.** The one member of a party who voted the other way from every other member of it, with at least ten of the party voting, at most one per party (the newest). The majority leader's vote with the prevailing side of a question the party lost is the reconsider switch Constituent Alignment also exempts, not a break. Drawn as one dot per member of the party voting, the breaker a ring, and said in words ("1 of 45 Democrats voted Nay").
- **A law enacted** from the start of that week to today (`congress_service.became_law`), and **a nomination confirmed** in that span (not a list, not military), with its confirmation vote where one is on record.
- **Comment periods.** The proposed or final rule whose comments close soonest, as a countdown with a link to comment, then the most salient other open periods (`comment_periods.salience`, paperwork notices left out).
- **The news.** The Action Center's recent issues (`action.recent_issues`), one per story: a national monitor's other days fold under its newest (`action.monitor_groups`), named beside it when in the same Monday-to-Sunday week, and the issue carries an "Ongoing story" tag linking the monitor. Each shows the outlet whose headline it is.

The lead is the first that holds, in this order: the closest vote, if its margin is smaller than the median margin of this Congress's simple-majority record votes (`notable.median_margin`, measured from the stored roll calls on each request, so the bar moves with the record and no threshold is typed); else the newest law; else the newest confirmation; else the newest lone break; else the day's top issue; else the comment deadline closing soonest. While Congress is out (`/congress`'s own recess test, `congress_service.recess`), a vote (the closest or a lone break) doesn't lead, since its week is the last one of business, not this one; it stays on the page, dated. The tier is the first remaining entry with a figure from Congress, then Agencies, then the news, two at most and never two from one column. Each column lists what is left of its own, three at most (a layout decision, not a rule about the data), each kind's first entry before any kind's second, and nothing appears twice. In a recess the dateline says Congress is out. With nothing on record at all, the section is left out rather than shown empty. The section is share-ready as an image (`data-share-section="record"`). A score's week-on-week "biggest mover" was considered and left out: a change is only comparable on one scoring method, and on 2026-10-13 none of 534 members had a comparable seven-day change, because methods change more often than that.

The homepage is ordered by how often each part changes (2026-10): the masthead, then "Entered into the record", the front page (see that section: a lead story, a tier of two, then Congress, Agencies and the news, rendered on the server), then "This week". Where the record comes from (the source ledger, the licenses, who runs it) is on the About pages, not the homepage, whose only statement of independence is the masthead's one line. The homepage has no index of the site's pages (removed 2026-10): it repeated the header's menu, which lists every section's pages and is in the server HTML, so every page stays crawlable from every page. The homepage asks the reader for nothing: the masthead is the statement of what the record covers, one row of links ("Your members", "Your ballot", the election's pages for its phase, "Congress this week"; with a state chosen in the header they go straight to that state's pages) and one plain line on who pays for it. Search is in the header on every page: SEARCH opens the site search (see Site search), a plain GET form to `/search?q=` underneath. The reader's state is asked once, in the header, and every page that needs one starts from it: held for the open tab only (sessionStorage), with an optional "Use my location" that works out the state on the device and keeps nothing else (AGENTS.md §8). The masthead's "For researchers" line is gone for the same reason. On a day Congress is out (the same test `/congress` leads with), the front page's dateline says so and its week link names the last week of business rather than reading as current. "This week" keeps every state's ballot link behind a disclosure, since the masthead's state list already leads to one. Measured under `next build` against the previous layout: the day's top issue moved from 5.7 phone screens down to 1.2 (7.65 s to 3.65 s by KLM) and into the first desktop screen (5.65 s to 2.65 s); keyboard stops on the whole page went from 176 to 100 at 1280 px (163 to 87 on a phone).

## This week

The homepage, and in a one-line form the pages most readers arrive on (a member's page, the daily Congress report and a bill's page), carry a "this week" strip from `GET /api/this-week` (`api/this_week.py`), rendered on the server with the page:

- **Election Day.** Days until the active election (`election_phase.active_election`; after it, the count), and every state's ballot page as a link. It is a list to pick from, never a field asking where the reader lives (AGENTS.md §8); the compact form keeps the list behind a disclosure.
- **Congress this week.** Whether each chamber is in session today, its record votes this week (the latest three listed, each linked to its bill), and the next meeting as the Record itself gives it (the newest notice on or before today, its date read from the Digest's or floor log's own wording). A week with no session reads "Congress is out until Tuesday, October 20"; a chamber with no notice on record says so.
- **Comment deadlines.** The next three on federal rules: rules an Action Center issue from the last two weeks cites first, then rules other documents cite, then any other rule, soonest deadline first within each. Notices (with them every information-collection and meeting notice) are left out by type, and so is a templated series: a Regulation Identifier Number shared by three or more open rules, as every FAA airworthiness directive shares one (on 2026-10-11, 79 RINs covered one open rule, 2 covered two, then 4, 4, 11 and 54). A series also inflates the citation count, since every document under the RIN reads as citing the rest. The compact strip's "next comment deadline" is the soonest of those same rules whatever its tier, a rule closing today included, on the deadline's own clock (Eastern): the first of the three can be a rule in the news days out while others close today.

Every sentence is a template around stored figures. When the backend can't answer, the strip is left out and the page renders as before. On a Congress day page about another day (a past day's report, or `/congress` when the latest sitting wasn't today), the one-line strip is headed "Now · Thu, Oct 15" (the API's `congress.today`, Eastern) rather than "This week": a reader on September 16's page read "This week … The Senate meets today" as September 16.

## Source ledger

The "On record now" list at the top of `/about/data#sources` (`components/about/SourceLedger.tsx`) gives each source with what it has supplied and how recent it is, from `GET /api/sources` (`api/sources.py`), counted from the stored records rather than typed: roll calls and sponsored bills in the sitting Congress, floor speeches, Federal Register documents, the Court's votes and opinions, and disclosed stock trades, each with its newest record's date. FEC filings carry no single date the site keeps, so that row gives the day the site last read the FEC and says "last read". A source with nothing stored shows 0 and "nothing on record yet" rather than dropping out; when the backend can't answer, the list says so and the sources' descriptions below it stand alone. It was the homepage's "Built from the public record" panel until 2026-10, when it moved there with the rest of the provenance: the homepage leads with the record itself.

## Compare

`/compare` sets two members of Congress side by side: both as markers on the same peer strips (`PairStrips`), and the money. Each side names the run and scoring version that stored its scores (`scoredOn`, `algorithmVersion` on the profile API, from the newest snapshot, `_scorecard_common.score_method`), and the pair has an APA reference naming the method. Two members of the same chamber also get how often they voted alike this Congress, from `GET /api/compare/agreement?chamber=&left=&right=` (`api/compare.py`): of the chamber's roll calls in the sitting Congress on which both cast a Yea or a Nay, how many they voted the same way on, as a sentence ("Voted the same way on 603 of the 723 roll calls this Congress where both cast a Yea or a Nay (83%).") and the backend's whole percent, then the five latest votes they split on, each with both votes and a link to its roll call. Housekeeping questions (quorum calls, motions to table, the previous question; `normalize_votes.is_housekeeping`) are left out, as Constituent Alignment leaves them out: they split on party lines by habit. A senator is matched to the roll calls the way a bill page links a Senate vote to a member (`bill_record.MemberLinks`): the roll call's surname as whole words of the senator's name (so a name ending "Jr." still matches), the state, and, where two senators of one surname share a state while both are on file, the LIS id the vote carries; a representative by bioguide id. Members of different chambers share no vote, so the section is left out for them.

## The chamber, measure by measure

`/congress/members/measures?chamber=senate|house` replaced the leaderboard (redesign 2026-10; it was at `/congress/members` until Congress › Members became the find-your-members directory, and a link there with `?chamber=` still lands on it). `/leaderboard` answers with a permanent redirect there, `?branch=president` to `/executive/presidents` and `?branch=scotus` to `/court`. It ranks no one and shows no composite score. Five strips, one per measure (breaks with party, PAC money, small gifts, bills past committee, and votes with the president), draw every serving member as a mark shaped by party (D circle, R square, independent diamond, so party never rests on colour), with the chamber median, and name the three members furthest above and below the figure expected for them (their seat's where the scorer has one, else the chamber median). Each row states the rule its words follow, served with it (`rule`, `ruleText`): the four peer measures follow the member pages' percentile rule; votes with the president (2026-10, `peer_comparison.president_row`, from `presidential_support_service`) has its own, since a senator has about 27 such votes against well over a hundred party-line votes: each member's 95% Wilson range against the seat's expected rate, "more than", "within" or "less than the seat's expected rate", no word under 20 counted votes (the member page's box follows the same minimum), and its callouts name only members whose whole range clears that rate. Its strip draws each party's median (dashed, in the party's colour, labelled under it) instead of the chamber median, which falls in the empty gap between the parties (76% in the Senate, October 2026), a rate almost no member has. Its readouts give the rate, the votes, the range, the expected rate and the comparison, and it links the full page with every vote and the president's stated position (`/congress/president/house` and `/senate`), which left the Members menu then (it stays at its address and in the sitemap, under By the numbers in the tab bar). The hub card that showed the House's overall rate went with it. Below, one table of plain facts (member, seat, small gifts, PAC share, breaks over party-line votes, bills past committee, passed, law, votes with the president over counted votes), alphabetical by default and sortable by any column (`aria-sort`), filtered by state, party and, in the House, seat lean from the sitting lines' Cook PVI (bands stated on the page as conventions). Data: `GET /api/congress/measures?chamber=` (`peer_comparison.chamber_measures`). `/compare` draws the same four strips with the two members as a filled and an outlined marker, beside the fact table, with no winner marks.

## Sharing a section as an image

A section marked `data-share-section` with a `ShareSectionButton` in it can be shared as a PNG, drawn in the reader's browser from the live page (`frontend/src/lib/shareImage.ts`; nothing is requested from another host). A picture travels without its page, so the frame says what it is: a strip naming the page's subject (a member's badge reads "72/100"), and under the capture where the figures come from, the section's link, the date the figures are as of, the scale any score is on, and the capture date ("Source: FEC filings; roll calls as the House Clerk records them; … · civitas-research.org/politicians/…#funding-independence · Data as of Oct 14, 2026 · scores out of 100 · captured Oct 15, 2026"). `ShareSubject.source` and `asOf` are required, so a page can't offer a picture without them; the as-of date is the backend's (a member's latest scoring run, the chamber's for the leaderboard strip, a vote's own date, the latest roll call read for a compare pair, the pinned Cook revision for a new map, a count's read time), or the capture day where the page reads its figures live. A section with figures of its own names them with `shareMetaAttrs` (a profile's holdings: the annual disclosure and its date; votes with the president: OMB's statements and the run's time; a race's live count: the state's feed). Until 2026-10 the frame printed only the link and "Captured {date}". Shareable: each scorecard column and summary, holdings, votes with the president, an issue and its facts, a bill's overview, summary and votes, a roll call, a ballot contest and the research on it ("What shapes this race"), a race's live count, the leaderboard's field strip, a compare pair's voting agreement, and a new House map's delegation by lean.

An issue's link preview (`/api/og?issue=`, the card a social post or chat app shows for an `/issue/` link) carries the same things the page says beside the story: the day, whose words the summary is ("— PBS NewsHour"), the photo's credit, the outlets or the one official record it comes from ("Sources: Roll Call, PBS NewsHour", "Source: Federal Register"), DEVELOPING for a story no outlet has covered yet, and the issue's own link (`issueCardTexts` in `frontend/src/app/api/og/route.tsx`). Until 2026-10 it carried the headline and the summary alone, with the photo uncredited and every story, news ones included, labelled "PUBLIC FEDERAL DATA".

## Fonts in the build

The build makes no network requests for fonts. The site's typefaces (Source Serif 4, Fira Sans, DM Mono and Archivo; SIL OFL) are committed under `frontend/src/app/fonts/`, one file per script subset, and loaded with `next/font/local`. `next/font/google` fetched them during every build, and about one build in five failed when Google answered with a URL Turbopack rejects. `backend/scripts/fetch_site_fonts.py` refetches them the way `next/font/google` did and rewrites `manifest.json` and `fallback.css`; `fonts.test.ts` fails if `fonts.ts` drifts from the manifest.
